\caption{Context-free grammar of \sys's scheduling language. $f$ and $g$ name
functions of the program, $q$ a queue the block declares, $p$ a parameter of $f$
of variant type, $v$ a variant's name, $i$ a loop index, $x$ an identifier, $n$
an integer literal and
$b \in \{\code{true}, \code{false}\}$. A schedule changes how the program runs
and never what it computes: every transform is a rewrite of the program's SSA
with the same meaning. The binds nest as the hardware does -- a \code{GPUThread}
loop inside a \code{GPUBlock} loop, a gang (\code{vectorize}) inside a
\code{CPUThread} loop, while under a GPU bind the warp is the gang -- and
\code{bind(TextureUnit, $\lambda$)} is the one bind of a function rather than a
loop: the lambda takes
$f$'s parameters and gives $f$'s value, and is what the unit computes in $f$'s
place. \code{reorder} is loop distribution followed by loop interchange: the
loops are named from the innermost out, as in Halide; what the outer body
did before its inner loop runs in a loop of its own before the nest, what
it did after in one after, pure arithmetic on the outer index is recomputed
in the new inner body and every other value it read is expanded into an
array of one entry per outer iteration; legal without analysis because a
\code{parfor}'s iterations are independent (Allen and Kennedy; Wolf and
Lam). \code{loopify} before \code{vectorize} gives each lane its own stack;
\code{vectorize} before \code{loopify} gives the gang one stack of nodes and
one of masks, a packet traversal. \code{defer} and \code{stage} are
defunctionalized continuations: a queue holds the continuation's varying data by
value, sized by the producer's trip count, and the drain is a \code{parfor} a
later directive may split, vectorize or bind. \code{defer} always captures the
complete continuation of the call -- the rest of $f$, and the queue owner's
frame; a caller between the two may not go on after its call -- unless the
call is a \code{spawn}, whose value only a reducer takes and whose
continuation does not wait. \code{stage} is \code{defer} of the continuation
alone, for work routed by the call's result. \code{specialize} is Halide's
\code{specialize} from a boolean to a variant: exhaustive copies of the loop
nest with the tag a constant in each; on a queue, the copies are of the
drained function, one queue and one drain per variant, and the push reads
the variant off the entry -- pbrt's queue per material type, as
\code{hits[Some].specialize(material)}. \code{skip} is not a transform but a
policy for a gang's branches (which arms get an any-lane test); the
\code{tree} declarations a block may carry belong to the layout language.}
